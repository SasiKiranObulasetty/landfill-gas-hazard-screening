## 2. In-Depth Pairwise Compatibility Assessments

### Pairing 1: GHGSat Methane Plumes + Carbon Mapper Airborne Plumes
- **Compatibility Classification:** **3 — Suitable only for exploratory or site-level comparison**
- **Geographic Overlap:** Overlaps across select major US landfill facilities (e.g., California and North American survey sites).
- **Temporal Overlap:** GHGSat covers 2021–2022; Carbon Mapper covers 2020–2021. Exact same-day temporal coincidence occurs on only a handful of flight dates.
- **Resolution Compatibility:** Mismatch in spatial resolving power (GHGSat ~25 m pixel vs Carbon Mapper ~5 m pixel). Carbon Mapper resolves individual wellhead/leachate riser leaks; GHGSat aggregates emissions across a broader facility sector.
- **Variable Compatibility:** Both measure instantaneous point-source emission rate in comparable mass units ($Q$ in t/h vs $q_{plume}$ in kg/h: $1	ext{ t/h} = 1000	ext{ kg/h}$). Both provide rigorous 1-sigma uncertainty estimates.
- **Main Compatibility Barrier:** Timestamp non-concurrence. Because airborne flights and satellite overpasses occurred at different hours or months, direct row-by-row correlation yields high scatter caused by true diurnal wind and operational changes.
- **Recommended Use:** Facility-level cross-instrument consistency analysis and multi-tier spatial resolution comparison (satellite screening vs airborne pinpointing).

### Pairing 2: GHGSat Methane Observations + Hyper-Landfill (EMIT / EnMAP / PRISMA)
- **Compatibility Classification:** **2 — Combinable after spatial/temporal matching**
- **Geographic Overlap:** High global overlap on super-emitting landfills (e.g., Norte III in Argentina, Kabd in Kuwait, Indian mega-dumps).
- **Temporal Overlap:** High overlap between 2022 and 2024.
- **Resolution Compatibility:** Excellent spatial alignment (~25 m for GHGSat vs ~30 m for PRISMA/EnMAP and ~60 m for EMIT).
- **Variable Compatibility:** Both platforms quantify column methane enhancement and invert emission flux ($Q$) using mass-balance/IME methods with GEOS-FP wind fields.
- **Main Compatibility Barrier:** Snapshot sparsity. Clear-sky overpass intervals mean each facility has only 3–15 quantified snapshots per year across all instruments.
- **Recommended Use:** Cross-sensor satellite calibration, multi-sensor plume detection benchmarking, and establishing annualized facility emission baselines.

### Pairing 3: Methane Observations (Satellite/Airborne) + NASA FIRMS / MODIS India
- **Compatibility Classification:** **4 — Not meaningfully combinable with currently available fields (Direct Join); 3 — Suitable for exploratory site-level comparison (Buffered Join)**
- **Geographic Overlap:** MODIS 2025 covers India nationwide. GHGSat and Carbon Mapper plume datasets are predominantly North American and global non-India (except for Indian facility coordinates in `landfills_hyper_obs.xlsx`).
- **Temporal Overlap:** Complete temporal mismatch between existing plume datasets (2020–2022) and MODIS active fire dataset (2025-01-01 to 2025-12-31).
- **Spatial Resolution Mismatch:** MODIS 1 km nominal fire pixel vs satellite methane 25–30 m plume footprint. A single MODIS thermal anomaly covers an entire landfill complex and surrounding industrial areas.
- **Main Compatibility Barrier:** Zero synchronized observations between available methane plume files and the 2025 Indian fire detections.
- **Scientifically Defensible Solution:** Spatial buffering. Extract MODIS 2025 fire detections within a 1.5 km buffer around known Indian super-emitter coordinates (Ghazipur, Bhalswa, Deonar from `landfills_hyper_obs.xlsx`). This allows analyzing seasonal fire frequency over Indian dumpsites, but cannot be correlated with 2021 GHGSat plumes on a timestamp-to-timestamp basis.

### Pairing 4: NASA FIRMS / MODIS + ERA5 Weather Reanalysis
- **Compatibility Classification:** **2 — Combinable after spatial/temporal matching**
- **Geographic Overlap:** ERA5 provides global grid coverage; MODIS covers nationwide India.
- **Temporal Overlap:** ERA5 reanalysis is available hourly back to 1940. (The local file `655880d7fc235e0aa45f8b8cbbf140f5.zip` contains 2,952 hourly steps for a sample box; full-year 2025 ERA5 can be spatially/temporally matched).
- **Resolution Compatibility:** ERA5 30 km reanalysis grid is interpolated to MODIS 1 km thermal anomaly detection points.
- **Variable Compatibility:** High physiological relevance. 2-meter temperature (`t2m`), 10-meter wind speed ($u_{10}, v_{10}$), relative humidity (from dewpoint `d2m`), and precipitation (`tp`) directly drive fire ignition and spread risk.
- **Main Compatibility Barrier:** Processing requirement to extract nearest-neighbor or bilinear-interpolated ERA5 hourly variables for each MODIS detection timestamp and coordinate.
- **Recommended Use:** Investigating environmental fire triggers (e.g., verifying whether Indian landfill thermal anomalies cluster during extreme ambient temperature (>40°C) and zero precipitation periods in pre-monsoon April–June).

### Pairing 5: Methane Plume Observations + ERA5 Weather Reanalysis
- **Compatibility Classification:** **2 — Combinable after spatial/temporal matching**
- **Geographic & Temporal Overlap:** Synchronizable across all satellite overpass timestamps and plume origin coordinates.
- **Resolution Compatibility:** Satellite methane inversions already rely on atmospheric boundary layer wind speeds from GEOS-FP or ERA5.
- **Variable Compatibility:** Wind speed and direction dictate plume advection and dispersion; ambient temperature and barometric pressure dictate microbial methanogenesis and cover soil breathing.
- **Main Compatibility Barrier:** ERA5 spatial grid (30 km) does not resolve localized micro-topographic wind tunneling around elevated landfill waste mounds.
- **Recommended Use:** Validating effective wind speed assumptions used in satellite flux quantification ($Q = 	ext{IME} \cdot U_{	ext{eff}} / L$) and modeling weather-driven emission fluctuations.

### Pairing 6: Satellite Methane Observations + Landfill Facility Inventories (Climate TRACE / US EPA LMOP)
- **Compatibility Classification:** **1 — Directly combinable after routine checks (for US via GHGRP ID; Global via Coordinates)**
- **Geographic Overlap:** 100% overlap for US facilities (LMOP covers 2,642 sites; Carbon Mapper and GHGSat target these exact sites); 100% global overlap via Climate TRACE (62,662 sites).
- **Temporal Overlap:** Multi-year facility operational records align with satellite survey years (2020–2024).
- **Resolution Compatibility:** Facility-scale mapping. LMOP facility boundaries enclose point-source plume origins.
- **Variable Compatibility:** Direct comparison between satellite-derived emissions ($Q_{obs}$) and regulatory modeled generation ($Q_{mod}$ from LandGEM / IPCC first-order decay).
- **Main Compatibility Barrier:** Definitional gap. Satellite detects instantaneous *fugitive surface emissions* escaping into the atmosphere; LMOP records total *gas generation* and *captured gas flow*. Mismatch reflects gas collection efficiency and oxidation in cover soils.
- **Recommended Use:** Core validation of multi-source consistency: demonstrating that satellite remote sensing exposes massive fugitive emission leaks uncaptured by conventional regulatory reporting.

### Pairing 7: Ohio / US In-Situ Gas & Thermal Data (ETLF + Bridgeton k6) + Satellite Indicators
- **Compatibility Classification:** **3 — Suitable only for exploratory or site-level comparison**
- **Geographic Overlap:** Bridgeton Landfill (Missouri) and ETLF Landfills A–E (Ohio/Midwest) are prominent US waste disposal facilities with known geographical locations.
- **Temporal Overlap:** Bridgeton monitoring covers 2013–2018; ETLF covers 2012–2019. Pre-dates high-resolution satellite constellations (GHGSat commercial fleet launched predominantly from 2020 onward; PRISMA launched 2019; EMIT installed 2022).
- **Resolution Compatibility:** Fundamentally different physical domains: in-situ internal extraction wells (subsurface depth 10–30 meters) versus satellite top-of-atmosphere radiance (surface / column integrated).
- **Main Compatibility Barrier:** Physical decoupling. Internal smouldering at 80°C inside a waste mass does not necessarily create a large surface thermal radiance anomaly if insulated by 2–5 meters of clay/geomembrane cover, nor does it generate high methane plumes (subsurface combustion converts CH₄ to CO₂ and CO, causing CH₄ to drop).
- **Recommended Use:** Independent ground-truth model training. Use Bridgeton and ETLF data to train and evaluate ML classifiers for *subsurface elevated-temperature risk* based on gas chemistry (CH₄, CO₂, CO, O₂, temperature). Use satellite data to detect *surface thermal and gas anomalies*. Compare the two paradigms conceptually rather than attempting an unsupportable row-level merge.

### Pairing 8: Ground In-Situ Measurements + Meteorological Data (Rajbandh k1, Bubanj k5, NY Mobile Monitoring)
- **Compatibility Classification:** **1 — Directly combinable after routine checks**
- **Geographic & Temporal Overlap:**
  * **Rajbandh (`k1`):** 289 consecutive months (2000–2023) of synchronized waste tonnage, Landsat Land Surface Temperature (LST), NDVI, NDBI, humidity, and wind speed for a single South Asian landfill.
  * **NY Mobile Monitoring:** >312,000 real-time observations with simultaneous 1-second ambient CH₄, 2m air temp, pressure, sonic wind speed, wind direction, and solar radiation along vehicle routes.
  * **Bubanj (`k5`):** Synchronized ambient air temperature, wellhead gas temperature, and gas concentrations (% CH₄, % CO₂, CO ppm) across 10 wells.
- **Variable Compatibility:** Complete physical consistency. Temperature (°C), pressure (mbar), wind (m/s), and gas concentrations (ppm or % vol) share uniform, standardized scientific units.
- **Main Compatibility Barrier:** None within each dataset. Across datasets, site-specific differences in climate (temperate New York vs continental Serbia vs tropical monsoon Bangladesh) must be preserved as distinct case studies.
- **Recommended Use:** Primary empirical foundation for analyzing how meteorological drivers modulate surface gas dispersion, internal heating, and seasonal thermal anomalies.
